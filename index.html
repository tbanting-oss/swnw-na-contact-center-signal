North America's Contact Center Market Is Splitting Into Two Very Different Races

On-premises still has a clear leader. On a primary-source basis, so does cloud — though the two aren't measured the same way yet.

p>
div>

North America's contact center market — CCaaS and on-premises combined — sits at roughly ~$3.7B–$3.9B in 2025. Split it in two, though, and the competitive picture changes completely. On-premises is a legacy stronghold: Avaya alone holds close to a third of it. CCaaS looked leaderless on analyst composites alone — but Five9's own audited filings put it ahead of the pack once isolated to pure-play subscription revenue, a bar its peers aren't yet measured against the same way. Over a third of the category still sits outside the named top five.

* Methodology & Data Hierarchy Note.span> Five9 Data Basis: Derived directly from audited SEC disclosures (FY2025 Form 10-K/8-K). Reflects pure software subscription revenue ($930.8M subscription base at 81% of total revenue). The US-only subscription floor of $826.5M establishes a conservative lower bound of 29.5%–30.6% against the $2.7B–$2.8B North American CCaaS baseline. True NA share is bounded above by 33.8%, assuming a similar subscription mix internationally — not independently confirmed. Peer Data Asymmetry: Peer vendor market shares (NiCE, Genesys, Amazon Connect) rely on desk research estimates and secondary market benchmarks. While NiCE's global CX-cloud revenue is verified at $1,869.7M (reflecting its 83.53% Customer Engagement segment split), its primary filings do not break out a North American-specific subscription line. AWS Connect revenue is unseparated within corporate AWS disclosures, and Genesys is privately held. Direct comparisons between SEC-derived pure-play data (Five9) and desk research composites carry natural boundary and reporting variances.
